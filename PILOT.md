# Pilot, governance and commercial validation

This is an execution plan, not evidence that these activities occurred. No interviews, field permissions, quotations or savings are fabricated.

## First pilot

One township operator, one non-potable landscape zone, one existing rainwater tank and one nursery partner. Assign a named manager, facilities recorder, independent meter reviewer and resident contact before field work. Obtain owner/route permissions and an appropriate current water-quality review for the intended use; the app's reference fields are not certification.

1. Record site geometry, permitted uses, tank/reserve capacity, real transport capacity, ready processed stock and instruments. Register gateway identity; test timestamp, unit, duplicates, outage and capacity errors with simulation labels first.
2. Gather at least 14 complete baseline days and document irrigation schedules, weather, soil and occupancy/activity differences. Calibrate the daily demand model; retain its error instead of setting confidence from visual plausibility. Water-use is daily interval consumption, not cumulative counter values.
3. Review the plan and actual costs. Human approval reserves the one intervention. Recheck quality/route expiry before transfer. Record physical work evidence separately from UI button presses. Avoid moving water or material without the responsible operator's approval.
4. Gather the matching 14 post/model-window days. The present app's imported comparison is against a model baseline; an independent analyst should examine actual baseline/control data and weather before interpreting impact. Use a matched untreated plot and soil-moisture observations if claiming mulch's effect. Stored rainwater substitution requires measured delivery/irrigation evidence; do not infer it from total potable-meter change.
5. Report raw readings, gaps, baseline error, predicted and observed values, costs and limitations. A short wet/dry window cannot establish annual savings. Negative net results remain valid findings. Do not translate credits, material kg or potential vehicle-km into carbon without a defensible method and local factors.

Energy pilot: approved flexible equipment only, unchanged total load, facilities staff checks comfort/duty constraints; compare non-overlapping 28–31-day actual daily import cycles and account for solar, occupancy, weather and tariff category outside the simple app comparison. The app does not control equipment. RideShare pilot: one pre-approved corridor/pair, bounded mission and voluntary consent. Self-attestation supports a prototype incentive flow; independent journey evidence, safeguarding, transport/privacy review and a funded reward programme are prerequisites for public operation.

## Acceptance evidence

| Question | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Can data be trusted? | Instrument/export reference, original time/unit, class, batch hash; cross-check readings, calibration and gaps. |
| Did the right person approve? | Manager identity, revision, frozen calculation, economic reason and consent/quality references. |
| Was work performed? | Actual staff evidence and independent review; a signed click alone is insufficient. |
| Were rewards counted once? | Booking → check-in → weighing or two-party trip trail; unique intervention/user award key; funded policy. |
| Was there useful impact? | Baseline/control method, paired periods, measured quantities, uncertainty and complete incremental costs. |
| Can the evidence be checked later? | Signed export and original fingerprint/checkpoint held independently; private operational backups. |

## Ownership, privacy and audit

The pilot operator owns site inputs and operational approval; providers own responsibility for their reported observations; the team provides software/model transparency; the independent reviewer evaluates outcomes. Choose actual named owners during pilot agreement. Use demo identities for public presentations. Collect only necessary account and mission information; no GPS/contact details are required by this prototype. Production retention/deletion/consent policies must reconcile privacy with financial/operational record obligations and be reviewed for the actual use case.

Local prototype stores one site and fixed bootstrap roles. Before deployment: organisation/site isolation, managed identity onboarding/revocation, HTTPS, scoped device credentials and rotation, access/retention controls, backups, monitoring and independent signing checkpoints. The current verifier is linear in audit history and reads a single-site database; it is not a multi-tenant scaling benchmark. A host administrator holding the private key can replace the entire identity; an independent original fingerprint is essential.

## Commercial hypotheses to test

Potential buyer: township/facilities operator. Initial offer: a paid pilot for auditable resource actions using existing workflows and instruments. Possible later pricing: per-site subscription plus integration/support fees. These are hypotheses, not validated prices or revenue.

Record actual benefit as avoided eligible costs and incremental work, separately from social/environmental outcomes. Include instrumentation, installation, calibration, maintenance, lab reviews, staff, transport, processing, rewards, hosting, insurance/administration where applicable. The UI's “additional cost” control permits a pilot allowance; zero does not mean those costs are absent. Do not annualise the dry reference model to pitch guaranteed ROI.

Interview an actual facilities manager, nursery/landscape operator and prospective resident participants. Record consent, date, role, current workflow, quantified pain, existing alternatives, adoption barrier and willingness to fund a pilot; report answers as evidence rather than writing fictional testimonials. Compare relevant products using their official documentation before claiming uniqueness. NEXUS's intended differentiation is routing a feasible resource action across operational and community roles with a source-aware outcome trail; uniqueness is unproven.

## Multiplication path

Start with Water + BioLoop; use the same source/review/action/evidence contracts for Energy and RideShare. Extend to a second township only after collecting measured baseline, integration effort and staff adoption evidence. Parameterise actual assets, permissions, tariffs, equipment, corridor and policy per operator. A credible expansion case is repeated measured value and lower onboarding effort; the current fixed-site code alone does not establish nationwide scalability.

Suggested pilot exit decision: proceed only when required source completeness is met, no unreviewed hard-constraint failure remains, evidence survives independent export verification, operating staff can complete the workflow, and actual incremental benefits/costs justify the next trial. Set numeric thresholds with the real pilot owner rather than inventing successful targets after results arrive.
